# Design

## Two levels, two lines

| Level                                                                                       | What crossing it changes                                                                        | What it does not change          |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Pattern level: the last confirmed high in a downtrend, the last confirmed low in an uptrend | Structure: one close beyond it breaks the pattern, which becomes undefined ("Sin patrón claro") | Regime: the trend stays as it is |
| Trend tipping point: the near Regime tipping close                                          | Regime: closes beyond it, over the confirming sessions, change the trend                        | Structure                        |

The pattern line speaks of one close ("Un cierre"), since one close is a Structure Break. The tipping point speaks of closes ("Con cierres"), since Regime needs its confirming sessions. That is also why "La tendencia seguiría siendo …" holds after the break even when the pattern level lies beyond the tipping point: one close does not change the trend.

The "seguiría siendo" clause is left out while Regime is `null`. In a mixed trend it reads "La tendencia seguiría siendo indefinida."

A mixed trend has no trend to stop being, so "dejaría de ser indefinida" would not say where it goes; "pasaría a ser alcista|bajista" names each direction.

## Emphasis

The figure the reader looks for (the peso figure on a peso line, the analyzed price otherwise) and the name of what the level changes are bold. Support and resistance keep their bold lead ("Soporte", "Resistencia") and an unbolded level.
